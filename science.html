The Science

What gets there

An active ingredient only works if it arrives where it is needed, at a usable concentration. Most of them do not. This is the part of skincare nobody puts on the front of a bottle — and it is the only thing EYVE is built on.

01 — The descent

What the vesicle has to get past

Three layers stand between a bottle and the tissue an active is meant to treat. Each refuses a different thing, and a carrier has to answer all of them.

01

A wall built of dead cells and lipid

The stratum corneum is twenty microns of flattened corneocytes mortared with ceramide, cholesterol and fatty acid. It is selectively permeable by design: small, oil-loving molecules cross it, and most actives are neither.

02

A carrier the wall is made of

The mortar holding that wall together is the same class of lipid a vesicle’s shell is built from. The barrier recognises the arrival rather than repelling it — which is why encapsulation changes what arrives rather than how much is applied.

03

Living tissue, and a clock

Below the barrier the actives meet water, enzymes and turnover. A free active that gets this far is spent quickly; a vesicle releases as its shell dissolves, which is what “uniform release” means in practice.

02 — The carrier

Why we say Neo-Liposomal

A liposome is a microscopic sphere formed from the same class of lipid that makes up your own cell membranes. Because the structures match, the barrier treats an arriving vesicle as something familiar rather than something to repel. Inside that sphere sits the active, shielded from air and light — which is why encapsulated formulations stay stable where free-form actives degrade in the bottle.

What the “Neo” is doing

Conventional liposomes are fragile and often break down before they reach anything useful. Our carrier is engineered for a tighter size distribution and greater stability, so more of each dose survives the journey and releases where it was meant to.

This is why our percentages look modest next to brands competing on concentration. A lower percentage that arrives beats a higher one that does not, and the number on the front of a bottle describes what went in rather than what got there.

What this does not mean

It does not mean results arrive faster than biology allows. Pigmentation takes eight to twelve weeks to shift. Collagen takes longer. Delivery improves how much of an active is usable — it does not change the timeline of skin renewal, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something.

Four jobs, one carrier
